Four contracting models and how to choose

The commercial model should follow the risk profile of the work.

The four models

A fixed price on an undefined scope protects neither party. It only delays the dispute.

  1. Fixed scope

    Defined outcome, agreed price, firm date. Best when requirements are clear and you need a fixed budget to get approval.

    Best for: well-defined MVPs, migrations and integrations

    • Detailed scope and acceptance criteria agreed up front
    • Milestone-based payments tied to demos
    • Change requests priced transparently
    • Fixed delivery date with agreed buffer

  2. Time and materials

    Pay for the capacity you use. Best when priorities will change, as they do on most product work.

    Best for: evolving products and discovery-heavy work

    • Two-week sprints with re-prioritisation each cycle
    • Transparent timesheets and burn reporting
    • Scale the team up or down as the roadmap shifts
    • Stop or pause with 30 days' notice

  3. Dedicated team

    A standing pod that works only on your product, using your ceremonies and tools. It operates as part of your engineering team.

    Best for: multi-quarter roadmaps and long-term ownership

    • You interview and approve every engineer
    • Fixed monthly cost per role, no hidden extras
    • Optional delivery lead, designer and QA in the pod
    • Team knowledge builds up over time

  4. CTO as a service

    Senior technical leadership without a full-time hire: architecture calls, vendor decisions, hiring support and a roadmap your board can read.

    Best for: funded startups and non-technical founders

    • Fractional engagement, typically 2 to 5 days a month
    • Architecture and build-vs-buy decisions
    • Technical due diligence support
    • Hiring plans, interviews and team structure

Terms common to every model

Always included

  • Named engineers you meet and approve
  • Sprint demos and written fortnightly summaries
  • Code in your repositories from commit one
  • Clear IP assignment in the contract
  • Documentation and handover materials as deliverables
  • A single point of contact from our senior team

What we will not do

  • Quote a fixed price on a scope nobody has defined
  • Staff the pitch with leads and the project with juniors
  • Withhold your code, credentials or cloud accounts
  • Bill for the time it takes us to fix our own defects
  • Go weeks without an update
  • Recommend a rewrite you do not need
