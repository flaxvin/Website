Pathayam: our own product, with published prices and public source.

Pathayam, envelope budgeting for Indian banking

Personal budgeting software sold in India is largely imported. It assumes a card-first, statement-first market and consequently handles local conditions poorly: high daily UPI transaction volume, multiple credit cards on differing cycles, and bank statements secured with PAN-derived passwords.

Pathayam is built for that. Every rupee that arrives is assigned to a job before it is spent, so the month is decided in advance rather than reconstructed afterwards. Spending on a card immediately reserves the cash to clear it. Statement PDFs from twelve Indian banks, password-protected ones included, are parsed into a review queue, checked against the closing balance, and a person approves every row before it posts.

The name is Malayalam (പത്തായം), the wooden chest that held a Kerala household’s harvest, filled once and drawn from with care.

At a glance

  • Envelope budgeting with rollover of every leftover and every overspend
  • Credit cards ranked by what falls due next, with the cash already set aside
  • Statement PDFs from twelve Indian banks, plus CSVs, bank alerts, CAS, and YNAB or Actual Budget exports
  • Holdings and net worth: FIFO lots, exact cost basis, XIRR; mutual funds, shares, FDs, PPF, EPF and gold
  • Your AIS read into the tax estimate, old and new regimes side by side
  • Ask Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini about your budget through a built-in MCP server, read-only unless you allow changes
  • One container and one SQLite file; no telemetry, ever
  • TypeScript
  • Node 24+
  • node:sqlite
  • node:http
  • Zero runtime dependencies
  • PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0

Three reasons we built it

  1. An unaddressed technical problem

    Indian banking is a genuinely awkward data-integration problem: inconsistent statement formats, password-protected PDFs, UPI volume, no clean API surface. No available product addressed it while allowing the user to retain custody of their own data.

  2. Accountability for our own decisions

    Operating a product obliges us to live with our own decisions well beyond the invoice: our migrations, our defect reports, our incident response. Standards applied only to other organisations’ codebases tend to erode; ours are not permitted to.

  3. Independently verifiable evidence

    Client work is largely subject to non-disclosure. Pathayam is not. The source and the test suite are public, and the engineering can be assessed before any agreement is entered into.
