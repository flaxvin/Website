Our product

Pathayam — envelope budgeting for Indian banking.

Personal budgeting software sold in India is largely imported. It assumes a card-first, statement-first market and consequently handles local conditions poorly: high daily UPI transaction volume, multiple credit cards on differing cycles, and bank statements secured with PAN-derived passwords.

Pathayam is built for that. Every rupee that arrives is assigned to a job before it is spent, so the month is decided in advance rather than reconstructed afterwards. Spending on a card immediately reserves the cash to clear it. Statement PDFs from eleven Indian banks — password-protected ones included — are parsed into a review queue where a person approves every row before it posts.

The name is Malayalam — പത്തായം — the wooden chest that held a Kerala household’s harvest, filled once and drawn from deliberately.

Why we built it

Rationale for developing it.

An unaddressed technical problem

Indian banking is a genuinely awkward data-integration problem — inconsistent statement formats, password-protected PDFs, UPI volume, no clean API surface. No available product addressed it while allowing the user to retain custody of their own data.

Accountability for our own decisions

Operating a product obliges us to live with our own decisions well beyond the invoice: our migrations, our defect reports, our incident response. Standards applied only to other organisations’ codebases tend to erode; ours are not permitted to.

Independently verifiable evidence

Client work is largely subject to non-disclosure. Pathayam is not. The source and the test suite are public, and the engineering can be assessed before any agreement is entered into.
